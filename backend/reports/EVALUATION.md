# Passage model: evaluation

Backtest of the inside-Parliament passage model (MODEL_SPEC §7). Generated by
`uv run python -m westminster_weather.model.cli evaluate`; numbers are in `metrics.json`.

## How it was tested

- **Rolling refits:** before each test session, every model is refitted on the bills that had
  finished before that session began (first refit: data up to 2019-10-14, sessions
  20-30, 2006-07 to 2017-19).
- **Tested on** 222 bills introduced in sessions 34-39
  (2019 onwards) that have finished; the current session is not scored.
- Each test bill was forecast on the day it entered each stage, as if it were that day:
  1,281 forecasts, 1,207 of them for bills that became law.

## Results

| | Model | B1 | B0 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Average error in Royal Assent date (days) | **61** | 83 | 107 |

- **The model beats both baselines on timing.** B1 uses the typical remaining time
  from the current stage; B0 the typical total time from 1st reading.
- **Errors fall as a bill progresses** (table below): this is what live updating is for.
- **Uncertainty windows are too narrow:** the 80% window contained the real Royal Assent date
  66% of the time (target 80%). Most misses are bills that
  took longer than forecast.
- **The chance of becoming law is too pessimistic:** forecasts averaged
  87% but 94% became law.
  The Brier score (0.0517) is worse than always forecasting the
  overall pass rate (0.0544).
- **Ranking is good:** concordance index 0.796 (0.5 = random, 1 = perfect)
  for which bills get Royal Assent sooner.

## What limits early forecasts

For the 193 test bills forecast at their first stage, the actual time to Royal Assent
(median 170 days) splits into:

- **waiting between stages** (from the last sitting of one stage to the first sitting of the
  next): median 117 days, on average 62% of the
  time; it accounts for 53% of the variation
  between bills;
- **time within stages** (mostly long committee stages): median 51
  days, 47% of the variation.

How late a bill turned out against its forecast goes with both: correlation
0.72 with waiting time and 0.84
with time within stages. In plain English: at first reading the model cannot know when the
government will find floor time for the next stage, nor how contested the bill will be in
committee. Both are decided later, by the business managers and by the debate, and the
data available at introduction (no votes, amendments or programme-motion detail) says
little about either. That is why early forecasts stay around 100 days out, while forecasts
from the Lords report stage onwards are within about a month.

## How forecasts are made now

- **Simulator** (10,000 paths): speed model with pace, a per-path speed spread (s tuned to 80%
  coverage on 2014-15 to 2017-19 only), future sitting days at the training clock's density,
  elections, and prorogations that kill, carry over or rush through an unfinished bill.
- **Dates** are the average of the simulator's and a LightGBM quantile model's (chosen on
  training-era sessions, not test sessions). Pass probabilities come from the simulator only.
- **Scheduled sittings are used only in live forecasts** (a later stage with a future-dated
  sitting is forced to that date). They are **excluded from every backtest**, because we do not
  know when each sitting was announced.

## Limitations

- **80% windows are still too narrow on 2019+ bills** (66%
  coverage): the spread tuned on 2014-19 gives 80% there, but bills since 2019 are more variable.
- **Early forecasts are limited by scheduling and committee length**, which are not known at
  introduction (see above).
- **Carried-over bills** are still forecast too early (about 180 days), because a future
  carry-over motion is rarely anticipated.
- **Session ends:** a prorogation kills an unfinished bill only with the historical rate
  (12% in its first House, 4% later); otherwise the bill is carried over or rushed through. The
  rates rest on 17 deaths, 10 of them at the October 2019 prorogation.
- **No vote, rebellion or Lords-defeat data yet** (BUILD_PLAN task 7 skipped): defeat risk is a
  fixed 0.2% per Commons vote stage.
- **Committee of the whole House** is only recorded separately by the Bills API from 2022-23,
  so the model cannot learn its effect.
- **Small sample** (about 590 bills, 66 failures): wide intervals are honest, not a bug.
- Pre-Parliament stages and commencement are not covered.
